The microwave beeps.

I take the cup out by the rim. The steam gets in my eye first. Then some splashes jump onto my hand as I pull it out. My body flinches from the pain.

The ER is full again. Beds fill the hallway. A man angrily shouting.

“Ms, I’ve been waiting for 3 hours and haven’t gotten a bed. Can’t you see I’m in need of motherfuckin’ emergency”

The man’s halo is green, almost like an aurora.

In the corner sits a quiet middle-aged man with a cyan halo.

I carry it to him slowly, so it doesn’t spill.

Stable. It sits there, steady, while he tries to sit up. His hands shake a little. I put the cup on the tray and push it closer.

I open it slightly so the steam comes out. Then I give him a plastic fork in his right hand and some napkins to his left.

He nods to me as if he were saying thank you. After a couple of bites he takes a sip of some juice he has. Orange juice with some pulp.

I sit down for a few minutes. My eye feels this burning sensation. Round like a volleyball, and red.

I close my eyes for a second. The pain swells in bursts. Waiting till I least expect it to attack.

The middle-aged man compliments how pretty my hair looks, that it reminds him of his granddaughter.

I nod and say thank you.

I watch him as he slowly eats and see his faint smile.

I step up to the front steps and open the fence. My shirt is slightly tugged by the fence as I brush by.

Shuffling through my keychain for the main key. I grab the dandelion yellow door knob and twist it left while I turn the key right.

I walk in a few steps inside. I bend on one knee and carefully untie my shoelaces to not make a knot. Slowly taking off each shoe and slipping into a pair of burgundy slippers.

In the living room my father seems aggravated. His face wrinkled like a ball of paper. Beads of sweat dripping down his face.

He seems to be looking for something.

I greet him softly, letting him know I am home. He asks me how work was. I tell him nothing eventful. I really want to say I am exhausted.

He asks me a second question after taking a deep breath. Then he places his hands on his hips. “Have you seen any money laying around the house?”

“No,” I say.

“When you get a chance can you help me look by checking around the house,” he says.

I nod hesitantly.

I change into my regular clothes, slipping my scrubs off and into a white T-shirt with Basquiat’s Abuelita. Grandiose figures. Fragile underneath. I put on some gray sweatpants I used to go jogging in.

I walk out of my room and begin to help my father search for his money.

I decide to start with the basement. I grab the wooden banister and start slowly stepping down. Each creak from the steps feels as though it could wake a sleeping bear. As I go down into the basement’s dim interior I notice a smell. Sour, strong, the type of smell you’d turn your nose away from if you smelled it on the street.

There is a cloth bag tucked nearby an old desk. The desk has been sitting for years without being used. I peer over ever so slightly to see what is inside the bag.

Cans and glass bottles, lots of them. The words alcohol content 40%, Smirnoff Vodka, and beside the bag is money laid scattered around.

I hesitate for a moment, then quickly pick up the scattered money when I hear my father’s rushed footsteps down the stairs.

I hide the money in the pocket of my sweatpants, sloppily. I start to hear a faint beat coming from my heart. The perspiration coming from my hands makes it feel as though anything I touch would slide right through.

His frustrated face begins to switch to one of suspicion.

“What were you picking up a moment ago,” he says.

“I was just cleaning up some garbage lying around.”

He opens his mouth and purses it in a way to emphasize the word “What.”

“What type of fool do you take me for? I saw you pick up the money.”

My voice begins to crack and turn high-pitched.

“I’m telling you the truth.”

“DO YOU THINK I’M SOME JACKASS!” he screams.

“YOU HAVE NO RIGHT TO HIDE MY MONEY FROM ME. GIVE ME MY FUCKING MONEY RIGHT NOW!”

His voice reverberates throughout the entire house. In fact, I’m sure the entire neighborhood feels his anger.

I reach into my pocket and grab the crumpled up money with my sweaty palms. I avert my eyes from looking directly into his eyes.

As I move closer to hand him the money he grabs my arm. With a grip so tight I want to cry. As I hold the tears back, he says these words in a lower voice than his screaming from before.

“If you ever try to hide or take something from me again. I will end your little ass.”

After hearing those words it is as if I am cattle and he has branded those words onto my brain.

The next day, Wednesday, at 11:40 pm

In the ER room a lady who appears to be in her late 30s with brunette hair comes in.

She has bruises on her lips with blood dripping profusely.

A swollen right side of her face as if she has been slammed against a concrete wall.

And her clothes are stretched and distressed in certain places.

Her halo has a shaky cyan color to it.

She approaches me to explain her situation. Her name is Louisanne. She tells me she has been raped by a man she was seeing for a short while after she rejected his advances.

Tears flow down her face like a waterfall. She has a slight shiver to her body.

I comfort her as we wait for a bed to be set up for her. She is put on the priority list for people who need beds because of her situation.

She tells me that she is afraid she might have contracted STDs. She is cautious of the man she was talking to because she has gotten word of him spreading it.

She slowly curls her head downwards and covers her face with one hand. As if she has felt embarrassment.

I repeat the words softer each time I say them.

I understand.

I understand.

I understand.
